The premise

Deep technology is forged under pressure.

The science is hard. The timelines are long. Capital is patient or it is gone, and the people building it are usually doing several jobs at once.

Advice is easy to come by. Advice from someone who has done the job is less so: someone who follows the science and the engineering, has built and scaled a company under the same pressures, and is at home in the boardroom and the policy room. That is the gap Crucible was set up to fill.

The difference

Two halves of the same problem.

Deep tech asks two things of an advisor at once: enough grasp of the science and the engineering to follow what is being built, and enough experience of running a company to know what building it will cost. Tom Fleming has spent his career on both sides.

He is an operator-scientist: a chemist by training who went on to build, finance, and run a company rather than advise one from the outside. That is what the practice rests on. He is not a career consultant, he works across the science and the business rather than one of them, and he does the work himself.
